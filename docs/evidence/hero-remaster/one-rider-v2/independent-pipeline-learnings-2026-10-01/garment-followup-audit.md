## Contact and interpretation

The saddle test clips actual posed lower-facing skin triangles into each of the 48 original saddle triangle prisms. It reports both raw vertical clearance contact [-.5,+1] mm and the solver's intended 2 mm cloth support band [1.5,3] mm. Contact area is a finite triangle-area measurement; clearances alone do not imply support. Crossing surfaces can overcount contact areas and are separately rejected.

The independent force check sums only exported positive force column 1 for finite offset gap <.5 mm; columns 0 (vertex ID) and 2 (gap) are never forces. Code exports the vertical spring-energy-gradient component, not a unit-saddle-normal dot product. This is a modeled cloth/skin-attachment load, not certification of support for rider body mass. The 2 mm offset is vertical: saddle normal Y ranges .509–.997, so it corresponds to only 1.017–1.993 mm along facet normals. Identical deformation matrices at hand/foot controls 8,12,15,18 and unchanged other primitives prove identical attached socket transforms; numerical solver geometry can still fail elsewhere.

The gate requires zero strict crossings at each sampled time. It does not prove continuous collision detection between samples, endpoint/tangential/coplanar nonpenetration, whole-body collision absence, physical closed volume, visual cloth quality, runtime/export parity, or real-time simulation. Coplanar candidates are explicitly unclassified and cannot pass. The single reserved solver revision belongs to the parent; this audit changes no solver parameters or source mesh.

## Primary research and practical implication

[Projective Dynamics, Bouaziz et al.](https://users.cs.utah.edu/~ladislav/bouaziz14projective/bouaziz14projective.html) presents local constraint projection with a global solve. Rest edge/bend springs are useful for local shape but do not alone establish nonpenetration. The experiment's separate contact projection is an additional approximation, whose measured residuals must be checked.

[Codimensional IPC, Li, Kaufman and Jiang](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.04457v3) couples strain, finite thickness and contact in one optimization, with conservative continuous collision bounds. The paper specifically identifies artifacts when strain limiting and contact are split. My inference from the code and measured regression: a metric step can pull distant folded patches through each other after a contact step separates them. The current solver checks endpoint edge crossings and projects them; it has no conservative bound along each deformation step. Its declared 2 mm thickness is therefore a response target, not a proven minimum nonadjacent surface distance. Applying a robust contact method would additionally require a feasible initial trajectory from the uncrossed source rest; an already-crossed posed target cannot supply that guarantee by itself.
