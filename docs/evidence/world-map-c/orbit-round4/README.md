The [silent played orbit and twelve tower taps](qualified-orbit-and-taps.mp4) exercise the **real review-only selector** at 932×430 landscape, DPR 2. [Front](qualified/front.png) and [reverse](qualified/orbit-3.png) frames come from that recording; [measurements](qualified/measurements.json) hold every sample and selected course ID. The comparison baseline is the [previous played orbit](../orbit-round3/qualified-orbit-and-taps.mp4) at the same viewport and DPR, judged against the user's [selected island image](selected-reference.jpg) during this round.

The prior island looked washed out: the trees lost depth, the quarry and front cliffs blended into the road, and the reverse angle showed a thin forest. I reduced ambient and fill light, eased the far fog and exposure, and grew deterministic conifer stands from 255→350 woodland trees and 120→145 snowy trees. Existing clearings and rear cuttings still expose the continuous 12-stop road from both sides. The route, towers, landmarks and navigation were not moved.

At DPR 2 the revised full orbit records **293–297 draw calls, about 449,100–449,400 triangles and 4–5 fps after the first frame** on headless SwiftShader, with zero page errors. All twelve tower taps selected C1–S3 in order. Compared with the previous DPR 2 scene's 395,030 triangles, the extra tree geometry costs roughly 54,000 triangles while draw calls stay nearly flat. There is no measured software-frame-rate gain. The screenshot is richer but still below the selected reference in model detail and shoreline/material finish. Physical iPhone frame rate, memory, touch and visual acceptance remain required before this can replace the production painted map.
