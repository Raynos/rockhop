## KEEP / STOP / CHANGE

**KEEP:** exact original selected assets/maps, useful editable work, unchanged
canonical backups, one coherent final wearer/rig, honest negative findings,
silent harness, native/engine replay and device requirements. Retain shared75
as a useful starting rig. Do not replace it merely to make progress appear new.

**STOP:** more global projection/registration variants, precision-normal work
before fixing silhouette, repeated packages, and treating source hashes or
successful modifiers as art. The rejected glove is not a local polish task.
Do not reopen dirty historical glove/jeans solvers as the next work. Do not
weaken final garment enclosure or promote a diagnostic assembly to players.

**CHANGE three construction rules:**

1. Preserve originals, not every vertex of the production derivative. The plan
   expressly authorizes new body/head/topology/rig. Permit a scoped neck, axilla,
   boot or glove region to be replaced, with new UVs and weights. Preserve facial
   identity and selected design, not the donor's defective throat geometry.
   Local body/rig changes are allowed when an observed anatomical defect requires
   them; their adapter/replay checks must then be updated. Source conservation
   is valuable provenance, not an artistic constraint.
2. Assemble a private working scene before final maps and four-weight delivery.
   Current uncommitted `production-assembly01/compose.py` requires every unit to
   be `actual-selected-baked`, all six garment meshes, final modifier/weight
   restrictions and packed maps. Those are delivery conditions imposed on the
   plan's early authoring scene. Bring the real selected sources and their
   current fitted derivatives together now, with status clearly visible. Keep
   actual original PBR where available and reference sources alongside unfinished
   regions; this is not the required fully textured moving milestone. Adjacent
   collars/cuffs/waist/boots must inform construction before polish.
3. A dense bake source does not itself have to enclose the wearer. Cycles casts
   from the low-poly receiver toward the high-poly source. For jeans, judge local
   source/target feature correspondence and ray coverage, with source regions
   isolated from the opposite leg, rather than pushing every dense vertex out
   of the body. This does **not** approve the current source or a blind whole-leg
   bake: crotch correspondence and stretched seams may need local reshaping.
   The 51 collapsed triangles/3,716 large normal rotations are a valid rejection
   of that OUTSIDE result, not evidence that original selected detail is unusable.
   [Official Cycles baking manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/cycles/baking.html).
