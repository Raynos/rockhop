The road, sea, mountains, snow, buildings, and flag towers rotate together with coherent parallax. The screenshot of the concept board is not used as a sprite or surface texture. The Blender tray improves front cliff depth and quarry strata in the matched mobile orbit; the broad silhouette and map interactions are unchanged. The result is still short of B's photographic miniature quality: tree crowns and rock faces remain angular, the ship lacks full industrial detail, water movement is minimal, and mobile GPU performance remains unmeasured. The latest 5.4 second readiness number is headless SwiftShader on this machine and does not predict an iPhone load time. [`NEXT-ASSET-PASS.md`](NEXT-ASSET-PASS.md) records the remaining path to reference fidelity. This standalone prototype is exploratory and is not promoted to the production map.

## Harbor silhouette pass

[`harbor-before-after-orbit.mp4`](harbor-before-after-orbit.mp4) puts the same played pointer orbit side by side at 844×390; left is the code and terrain GLB at commit `3255b375`, right is the new pass. Each paired frame is captured after the same pointer movement and after both cameras reach the same azimuth; separate browser video clocks gave an out-of-phase comparison and were discarded. [`harbor-before-after.jpg`](harbor-before-after.jpg) arranges camera-matched front, three-quarter and reverse views in rows (old left, new right). [`harbor-after-interaction.webm`](harbor-after-interaction.webm) includes a fresh orbit followed by twelve actual tower clicks. [`harbor-pass.json`](harbor-pass.json) records the angles, maximum paired-camera difference, errors and 1–12 selection results. The reproducible headless capture is `prototypes/world-map-b/capture-harbor-pass.mjs`; its temporary baseline files are deleted after use.

The harbor ship is now broader and deliberately placed broadside, with modeled sheer, gunwale, bridge, cargo hold, railings, boom cables and a larger crossed-lattice shore crane. The sea has a darker shoal gradient and a low-amplitude traveling swell; the Blender coast material tint is less pale. A second pass routes the forest road onto the visible front slope, gives it dark asphalt and fine edge marks, clears nearby tree crowns, and adds faceted forest headlands and cliff ribs. The Blender terrain GLB was regenerated to follow the new road corridor. The opening view now shows more of the road from the harbor through the forest and preserves the twelve tower hit targets. No external art was added: new geometry, colors and motion are authored in `main.js`; the GLB is generated from `blender/build_terrain.py`. Existing texture sources retain their recorded provenance in `prototypes/world-map-b/textures/SOURCES.md`.

The visual gap remains large. The ship is still assembled from simple meshes and has no sculpted hull, baked rust, deck hardware atlas or convincing interaction with the quay. The crane is a thin lattice; the water has no shoreline foam shader; tree crowns remain repetitive; and the front forest slope is sparse after opening the road corridor. The cliff ribs add facets but do not replace a detailed sculpt with baked normals and ambient occlusion. The quarry, snow and global lighting remain below the reference's dense miniature quality. Only headless Chromium/SwiftShader was measured. A physical iPhone frame-time and load test is still required.
